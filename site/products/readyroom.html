ReadyRoom · Attendance and accountability

Available

Know who’s in, without a sign-in sheet.

ReadyRoom turns the Windows sign-ins your people already make into a daily picture of who is in, who is away, and who hasn’t been accounted for — organized by the directory you already run, and escalated to the right manager when someone doesn’t show.

How it works

Nobody has to remember to check in.

  1. People sign in to Windows as usual. A lightweight PowerShell agent, run by four Windows scheduled tasks, reports sign-in, screen lock, screen unlock and sign-out. Once IT has installed it there is nothing for anyone to open or click.
  2. Events become a working day. The first sign-in or unlock of the day counts as arrival. Locks, returns and departures fill in the rest, alongside any status someone has put on their calendar, such as work from home or leave.
  3. The directory supplies the people. Users, groups, managers and org structure are read from LDAP or Active Directory, so there is no second roster to keep in step.
  4. Everyone sees what they need. Individuals see their own day, managers see the people who report to them, and leadership sees organization-wide figures.

A status for everyone, every day

From the workstation

  • InSigned in and at the workstation.
  • AwayScreen locked.
  • OutSigned out for the day.
  • NoneNo activity recorded yet today.

From the calendar

People drag across days on their calendar to set a status ahead of time, and it is shown in place of workstation activity — so someone who is TDY is accounted for, not missing.

  • Work from home
  • TDY
  • Out of office
  • Scheduled leave
  • Late arrival
  • Early leave

Managers can also record unscheduled leave and excused or unexcused absences when they follow up on an alert.

Built for the whole chain of accountability

Individuals

Their own day, at a glance

  • Today’s status, the last 30 days and what’s coming up
  • Set a usual login time, or let their average arrival stand in
  • Name additional managers who should hear if they’re not in
  • Leave notes for the people who look them up

Managers

Who’s accounted for on the team

  • Accounted-for rate and who isn’t in yet, on the home page
  • Absence alerts waiting for a response, by email and in-app
  • Direct reports from the directory in one table
  • Group rollups and day-by-day, person-by-person reports over a date range

Leadership & administrators

The organization in numbers

  • Accounted-for rate, status mix and activity through the day
  • Breakdowns by organization, employee type, location and branch
  • Workstation agent coverage, so a silent agent isn’t mistaken for an absence
  • Organization calendar and holidays, and a date picker to see how any past day stood

Absence alerts that hold people to the right time

If someone hasn’t checked in by their expected time plus a configurable grace period, their managers are notified by email and under the bell in the app, and can record what happened. The expected time is worked out the same way everywhere it appears, so what a person is told matches what they are held to.

  1. 1st
    A late arrival they entered for today
  2. 2nd
    Their preferred login time from their profile
  3. 3rd
    Their average arrival from past check-ins

No alerts for days nobody is expected. Weekends and organization holidays are skipped, as are days covered by an excused status, and nobody is alerted about twice in one day.

Also in the box

Idle sessions close themselves

A scheduled job checks out sessions left open past a configurable idle threshold, so a forgotten sign-out does not become a 14-hour day.

Org chart from the directory

Hierarchy is derived from a flat LDAP attribute — RYS34B becomes RYS → 34 → B by default — with regex rules in Settings for codes that don’t fit.

Groups and search

Browse the groups you’re allowed to see, star favorites to filter down to them, and find anyone by name.

Classification banners

Configurable banner text and colour across the top and bottom of every page, for deployments that require a visible handling marking.

Security and privacy

Designed for environments that take identity seriously.

Certificate-first sign-in

Mutual TLS with X.509 client certificates, resolving the full subject DN against the directory. Browsers without a certificate fall back to an LDAP-validated form login.

Optional Windows sign-in

On domain-joined PCs the agent can check in with the user’s Kerberos ticket instead of, or ahead of, a certificate. Off by default; certificates keep working unchanged.

Who can see whom

User, manager and administrator roles, plus per-group grants where a role is too coarse, so a manager sees exactly the groups they are entitled to.

Counts, not names, in metrics

Organization-wide figures are aggregates and never list individuals.

Your data stays yours

Self-hosted. The directory stays the source of truth, and attendance records live in your own PostgreSQL database.

One deliberate exception. Sign-out runs after the user profile — and with it their certificate store — is already gone, so it cannot authenticate. Sign-in caches the server-issued session identifier and sign-out echoes it back, which lets the server recover the identity without a certificate.

What you need to run it

ReadyRoom technical summary
Component Detail
Server Spring Boot 4 on Java 21 with a Thymeleaf web UI, shipped as a container on Red Hat UBI 9
Directory LDAP / LDAPS or Active Directory, with pooled connections and paged searches for large directories
Database PostgreSQL for check-ins, statuses, roles, permissions, notes and calendar events
Workstations Windows with PowerShell; one install script registers the agent’s four scheduled tasks
Authentication A certificate authority for client certificates, and optionally an AD service account and keytab for Windows sign-in
Email An SMTP relay for absence notifications
Scale Query performance measured against a seeded 5,000-user, 90-day dataset of about 1.2 million check-in records
Configuration Environment variables or external config — no edits to a shipped file
